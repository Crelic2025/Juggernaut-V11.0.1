What This App Is

A 16-week powerlifting program tracker that tells you what weight to put on the bar, how many sets and reps, how long to rest, and tracks progress. The program is The Juggernaut Method 2.0 with the 10s and 8s waves inverted (many sets of few reps, 10×5 instead of 5×10) per the JTS inverted article; the 5s and 3s waves follow the book's standard table.

On top of the program, the app checks how you're feeling before workouts, can trim volume when you're beat up, and summarises each session. Rules decide; the coach explains. No coach — the app's own or Hermes — sets weights, training maxes or deloads; anything a coach proposes needs your Apply tap and passes the app's own checks first.

Book, JTS Article or App Rule

Every rule in this guide is tagged with where it comes from, and the coaches use the same tags:

  • Book — The Juggernaut Method 2.0 by Chad Wesley Smith.
  • JTS article — Chad Wesley Smith's articles on jtsstrength.com (the inverted 10s/8s waves come from here).
  • App rule — this app's own policy, not part of the method. Examples: readiness set drops, fire days, guided warmup changes, the missed-standard flag and "Reduce TM 5%", the take-a-week-off advice.

What's New in v14

  1. Book program table. 10s and 8s inverted (JTS article); 5s and 3s back to the book's standard table, including 5s intensification 4×5 and 3s accumulation 7×3 / intensification 5×3, with the book's lead-in sets. Only the standard 16-week mode remains.
  2. "+" sets. The last set of every accumulation and intensification week is a "+" set: hit at least the prescribed reps, then stop 2–3 reps short of failure in accumulation and 1–2 reps short in intensification. It is not a max test (book).
  3. The training max is the stored number. Settings shows and edits the TM; the 1RM beside it is for reference.
  4. The book's 5% rule decides how the TM moves after a test (see How the TM Moves).
  5. Test day: warm-ups never include a single at the test weight, and deload weights use the TM from before the test-day increase (book). Backoff sets after the test are gone.
  6. Weight actually lifted. Tap a set's weight to change it; the app asks why with one tap and keeps both the planned and the lifted weight.
  7. Cycles. "Start next cycle" and "Wipe history and start over" at the bottom of Settings; history is no longer deleted automatically.
  8. Hermes. Sessions can go to your Hermes bridge, which sends back suggestion cards and can power the in-app coach.

Fire Days — When You're Ready for More

App rule. A fire day is the flip-side of fatigue management. Fatigue management drops sets when readiness is low (<15/30). Fire day offers an extra set when readiness is exceptional (23+/30).

How it works: after readiness, if your score is 23+/30 the app asks whether to add one set at the same weight and reps as your last working set. Accept adds it; decline keeps the prescription.

Never on: test days (realization) and deload weeks. Readiness set drops don't run on those days either.

Guided Warmup Feedback

App rule. Before your first working set the app can walk you through a warm-up ramp with plate breakdowns and a one-tap rating per set.

Accumulation and intensification days: about 40%, 60% and 80% of the day's first set. Test days: the book's ramp for that wave (it never includes a single at the test weight).

Ratings: 💩 worse than expected · 👍 as expected · 🔥 stronger than expected. Only the last 2 ratings count: two 🔥 add one increment to the working sets, two 💩 take off two increments, anything mixed changes nothing. A change made this way is logged as a weight change with the reason "warmup feel".

Never changes test-day or deload weights. Turn it off in Settings → AI Hybrid Layer → "Guided warmup with emoji feedback."

Training Profile & Individualized MEV/MRV

App rule. The Lift Intelligence card's MEV/MRV targets (Minimum Effective Volume / Maximum Recoverable Volume) adjust to a 5-field profile in Settings.

  • Age bracket: under 25 / 25–34 / 35–44 / 45–54 / 55+. Older lifters recover slower → lower MRV.
  • Years of serious training: beginners handle less volume; seasoned lifters have higher work capacity.
  • Bodyweight: informational for now.
  • PED-assisted: raises the MRV ceiling about 10%.
  • Lifestyle stress (1–5): self-reported. High external stress reduces MRV.

The app combines these into a multiplier clamped between 0.75× and 1.25× of the defaults. The defaults (25–34, 3 years, stress 3, no PEDs) give exactly 1.0×.

Settings — Do This First

Program: units lb, bar weight 45, rounding 2.5. Pick your 1RM Formula (Epley, Brzycki or RPE-based) — it's used for estimates such as the calculator, not for TM changes. "Start next cycle" and "Wipe history and start over" are at the bottom of Settings, under Save All Settings.

Lifts & Maxes: enter your training max (TM) for each lift. The TM is the stored number every prescribed weight comes from; the 1RM shown beside it is TM ÷ 0.9, for reference. Your goals (for example 600 deadlift, 405 bench) live here as well.

Barbell Profiles: per-lift bar weight, collar weight, rounding and plate inventory, used for exact plate breakdowns.

Rest Timers: a wave×phase grid. The 8s intensification default is 105 s (JTS article; the book says 115 s). The 5s/3s defaults are the app's own (app rule; no source gives values). "0" = no auto-timer.

AI & Readiness: daily readiness check and RPE logging on/off. An OpenRouter key is optional and only a manual alternative to Hermes — one review never goes to two providers.

Hermes: the bridge address and token (Save & connect), "Send to Hermes" (off pauses sending; packets keep queuing), "Use Hermes for the in-app coach" (disabled, with the reason, until Hermes reports its coach is available) and the status line: Synced, Pending, Paused or Needs attention.

UI & Performance: Performance Mode turns off blur and animations on older phones.

Gym Equipment: six toggles (Barbell, Dumbbells, Cables, Bands, Machines, Pull-up Bar). Swap lists only show what you can do; an exercise that needs equipment you've switched off gets an amber warning instead of disappearing.

Props & Implements: boards, blocks, box, adjustable bench, pins, ab wheel, dip bars, foot anchor, SSB, push-up handles. Switch one off and the exercises that need it drop out of swaps and suggestions. "No prop-assisted exercises" switches them all off at once.

Estimating Your 1RM

On a fresh start the app asks for your 1RM and sets each TM to 90% of it (book). If you've never tested a max:

Option 1 — use a recent heavy set. If you can bench 185 lb for 5, open the calculator (header icon), enter 185 and 5, and use the estimate.

Option 2 — be conservative. Use a weight you could do for 3 slow, controlled reps as your 1RM. Starting light is better than starting heavy; the first test day corrects it.

Don't inflate the numbers. Every prescribed weight comes from the TM. Too high and you grind reps, miss rep standards and the progression stops working.

The 16-Week Program

Four 4-week waves — 10s, 8s, 5s, 3s — each with the same weekly rhythm: Accumulation (volume), Intensification (heavier, fewer reps), Realization (test day) and Deload (recovery; never skipped). Percentages are of your TM.

Deload weeks: 40%, 50% and 60% × 5, worked out from the TM you had before that wave's test-day increase (book).

"+" sets: the last set of every accumulation and intensification week (book). Do at least the prescribed reps, then stop 2–3 reps short of failure (accumulation) or 1–2 short (intensification). It isn't a max test. The 2012 JTS article used straight sets for the inverted waves; the "+" set there is Reese's choice. Each "+" set shows a rep goal from the same week of your last cycle.

The timeline bar shows all 16 weeks — blue glow = current, green = done, faded = upcoming.

Scheduling Your Training Week

You train 4 sessions per week — one lift per session. Each session you pick one of your four lifts (Squat, Bench, Deadlift, OHP), do the main lift, then that lift's accessories. A typical split: Monday Squat · Tuesday Bench · Thursday Deadlift · Friday OHP.

Two habits help: avoid training two days in a row, and separate squat and deadlift by at least two days. A program week means completing all 4 lifts once; when every lift chip is checked, the program moves to the next week. Order within the week doesn't matter.

Dashboard

With no session active, the dashboard shows where you are (cycle, wave, phase), which lifts are done this week (tap a chip to preview), and the workout preview with weight, plates, scheme, rest and percentage. Last Session shows your most recent result for that lift, including the test result on test days.

Cards that can appear: a TM confirmation after a test lifted at a different weight than planned (Apply TM change / Keep TM); Hermes suggestion cards with Apply and Dismiss; the deload banner; goal ETA cards once a lift has 3 tests; and, at the end of a cycle, Start next cycle.

Timeline Bar

The pills at the top show all 16 weeks. Tap any week to preview its weights for every lift; "Back to current" returns. The Program Overview chart bars do the same.

Lift Intelligence

Per-lift weekly hard sets against the MEV/MRV targets for that phase (warm-up and ramp sets excluded). Your Tier 1 accessories are the volume lever.

Weekly Plan

Lifts done vs. remaining, days left, a pace badge, and a "Next up" suggestion that respects squat/deadlift spacing.

Daily Workout Flow

Readiness Check

Tap Start Workout and rate Sleep, Motivation, Nutrition, Soreness and Performance 1–5 (a "Same as Last" button fills them from your last session). The app turns them into a 0–30 score. App rule: below 15 drops one working set, below 8 drops two; "Train Full Volume Anyway" overrides. Never on test days or deloads.

Logging Sets

Tap a set row to log it when the reps match the target, or use the hero card's stepper. The hero card shows the weight, a colour-coded plate diagram, target reps and % of TM.

Changing the Weight

Tap a set's weight to change it. The app asks why with one tap — felt heavy, felt light, plates or equipment, pain, short on time, other — and keeps both the planned and the lifted weight. A change can apply to the rest of the work sets too.

RPE

Optional on "+" sets and the test. RPE 8 = 2 reps left, 9 = 1 left, 10 = nothing left. It's recorded for review only — RPE never changes main-lift loads.

Rest Timer and Session Timer

The rest sheet counts down and previews the next set's weight and plate changes. Tap the session timer badge to pause/resume; paused time isn't counted.

Mid-Session Check

After 3 working sets: "How's the movement feeling?" — Sluggish / Normal / Dialed In. Stored for the coach.

Test Day (Realization, Week 3 of Each Wave)

Ramp sets first (the book's ramp for that wave, shown with blue "RAMP" pills; never a single at the test weight), then one AMRAP at the test weight: 75% (10s), 80% (8s), 85% (5s) or 90% (3s) of your TM. The rep standard is 10, 8, 5 or 3.

This AMRAP is the only max test in the program. If you enter more than 8 reps over the standard, the app asks you to confirm the number (a check against stepper slips, not a limit).

No readiness set drops, fire days or warmup weight changes on test day. The test result decides the TM (next section).

How the TM Moves

After a test (book):

  • Extra reps = reps − standard, at most 10. Zero or fewer extra reps: the TM is held.
  • Full step = extra × 5 lb (squat, deadlift) or extra × 2.5 lb (bench, OHP). Half step = extra × 2.5 lb (lower body) or extra × 1.25 lb (upper body).
  • The book's 5% rule: projected max = weight × reps × 0.033 + weight. Use the full step if projected ÷ (TM + full step) ≥ 1.05; otherwise the half step if projected ÷ (TM + half step) ≥ 1.05; otherwise no increase.
  • The weight is the one you actually lifted. If it differs from the plan, the increase waits for your OK on the dashboard (Apply TM change / Keep TM). It applies only to the TM it was worked out from: if the TM changes first (a Settings edit, a later test, a cut), the card says so and offers Keep current TM instead.
  • The deload that follows uses the TM from before the increase.

Missed rep standard (app rule — the book has none): the TM is held and the miss is flagged. After 2 misses in a row on one lift, the app offers "Reduce TM 5%" (TM × 0.95). It checks the evidence each time: one cut per new set of misses, and another cut for that lift needs a new test after the last cut. The old TM is kept in Settings → Coach Actions for a one-tap revert, as long as the TM is still the one the cut left (after a later test, a Settings edit or another cut the entry shows "stale", so a newer TM is never overwritten; undo the newest cut first). There's no repeat-wave option.

Estimated 1RM comes only from test days. 10s and 8s tests are marked "lower confidence"; deloads are left out of every trend.

Cycles, Wipe and Reset

Start next cycle (bottom of Settings, or the dashboard when a cycle is complete): back to week 1 with your current TMs carried into the next 10s wave. History is kept and every session keeps its cycle number, so "+" sets and tests compare with the same week of earlier cycles. Accessory weights, progress and weak points are kept. One exception, as at every new week: Tier 1 lifts from the library follow your TM and the wave, so they are recalculated for week 1 (a Tier 1 weight you set by hand lasts until the week turns over, and a new cycle counts as a new week, even when you start it from week 1). Exercises you added yourself and Tier 2 and 3 keep their weights.

Before a new cycle (app suggestion, not from the book): a week off or a light week of general fitness can help clear 16 weeks of fatigue. If you missed rep standards, the app's "Reduce TM 5%" (app rule, above) is the built-in way to restart lighter; you can also edit any TM in Settings.

Wipe history and start over: asks "Export first?", then erases sessions, accessory logs and progress, weak points, PR records, coach notes and chat, the coach action log, wave summaries and TM-cut blocks. It keeps your TMs (and the 1RM beside them), goals, everything in Settings and your accessory choices, and goes back to week 1, cycle 1. Accessory weights go back to the app's starting weights from your TM, also for a library exercise you renamed; exercises you added go back to the last weight you typed (0 if none). A library exercise renamed in v13.2.2 or earlier can't be matched to the library: the confirmation names it and its weight is kept.

Reset Everything (the reset icon in the header) also erases Settings, including the Hermes address and token. Enter them again to pair this phone with Hermes again.

Nothing is deleted automatically any more: history is kept however long it gets. Only the smaller backup copy in the browser may be trimmed if storage is full.

Coach and Hermes

After every session the app shows its own rule-based summary at once ("Coach: rules"). With Hermes's coach switched on, Hermes's review replaces it when it's ready — even if you close and reopen the app — labelled "Coach: Hermes · model".

Number check: every number in a coach reply must come from your data. A reply that fails is retried once; if it fails again you keep the rule-based summary. Suggestion cards that fail the check are dropped.

Suggestion cards can propose only: swap an accessory, ±1 accessory set, Reduce TM 5% (only through the evidence check), pull the deload forward, or add a note. Each needs your Apply tap and passes the app's checks; every applied card is in Settings → Coach Actions with a Revert button.

Coach tab: chat through Hermes, or through your own OpenRouter key as a manual alternative. There's no automatic fallback to a paid API.

What goes to Hermes: a packet after every session and a weekly catch-up, over HTTPS with your token. If Hermes can't be reached, packets wait in a queue (at most 500) and go when it's back. With no bridge set up, nothing is sent.

System Philosophy

What Is the Juggernaut Method?

The Juggernaut Method was created by Chad Wesley Smith, founder of Juggernaut Training Systems. It combines wave loading with submaximal training: strength is built by accumulating quality reps at submaximal loads, not by testing your max every week. The method controls how much stress you apply, when you apply it, and when you back off.

What Is the Inverted Version?

The book's 10s wave uses sets of 10 and the 8s wave sets of 8. The inverted 10s and 8s waves from the JTS article flip this: 10 sets of 5 and 8 sets of 5 (then 10×3 and 8×3). Same work, but every rep stays in the quality zone. In this app only the 10s and 8s waves are inverted; the 5s and 3s waves follow the book's standard table.

Why Waves?

Waves move from higher reps to lower reps: the 10s wave builds work capacity, the 8s bridges to strength, the 5s builds strength, the 3s peaks it. Each wave ends with a test that moves the TM.

Why Accumulation → Intensification → Realization → Deload?

Accumulation applies volume at moderate weight. Intensification drops volume and raises the weight. Realization tests what you built with one AMRAP. Deload lets you absorb the training. Stress → adapt → test → recover.

Why a 90% Training Max?

Starting the TM at 90% of your max (book) means every prescribed weight is one you can handle on a bad day. The test-day rule then moves the TM by what you actually showed, and the 5% rule keeps the projected max at least 5% above the new TM.

The 3-Tier Accessory System

Tier 1 (Supplemental, 5–8 reps): barbell variations for where your lift breaks down (Pause Squats for the bottom, Close-Grip Bench for lockout). Tier 2 (Hypertrophy, 8–15): build the prime movers and keep push/pull balanced. Tier 3 (Core/Prehab, 10–20): keep you training consistently.

Why Double Progression for Accessories?

Hold the weight and build reps; when every set reaches the top of the range, add the increment and start again. Simple and self-regulating.

How the Defaults Are Selected

Each lift's defaults cover both sides of the lift (bottom position and lockout), balanced push/pull in T2 and prehab in T3. The category next to each exercise says when to use it.

Your Workout: Start to Finish

Step 1 — Check the Dashboard

The Workout tab shows the cycle, wave, phase and week (e.g. "5S · Intensification · Wk 10/16"), the lift chips (green check = done this week), the workout preview with plates, your last session, Lift Intelligence and the Weekly Plan. Any TM confirmation or Hermes card waits here.

Step 2 — Start Workout

Tap Start Workout and rate readiness. Below 15/30 the app drops a working set (app rule; not on test days or deloads); "Train Full Volume Anyway" overrides. 23+/30 offers a fire-day set (app rule).

Step 3 — Warm Up and Work

If guided warmup is on, rate each warm-up set. Then work through the sets: lead-ins first on 5s/3s intensification days, then the working sets. Tap a row to log it, or tap the weight to change it and pick a reason.

Step 4 — The "+" Set

The last set of an accumulation or intensification day. Do at least the prescribed reps and stop 2–3 short of failure (accumulation) or 1–2 short (intensification); the rep goal from the same week last cycle is shown. Enter the reps you did.

Step 5 — Test Day

On week 3 of a wave: the book's ramp, then one AMRAP at 75/80/85/90% of TM. Enter your reps; the result decides the TM by the book's 5% rule.

Step 6 — Complete Session

The completion screen shows the summary (with the test, the "+" set and the TM change on test days), the coach summary, session difficulty, a notes box and "Where did you struggle?" for accessory targeting.

Step 7 — Accessories

Tap "Continue to Accessories →", log each exercise with the steppers, swap with ⇄ if needed, then "Finish Full Session".

If You Need to Leave Mid-Workout

Close the app at any point; the session is saved. Next time you open it, a resume prompt shows the lift and set progress.

Installing as an App (PWA)

iPhone (Safari): Share → "Add to Home Screen." Android (Chrome): menu → "Add to Home Screen" or "Install App."

Your data is stored on the phone (IndexedDB, with a backup copy in the browser). Train from one place — the home-screen app or a Safari tab — because on iPhone the two can keep separate data.

Resuming Unfinished Sessions

If you close the app mid-workout, the session is kept. Next time, a prompt shows the lift, set progress and readiness score: "Resume" picks up where you left off.

A 16-week powerlifting tracker built on Chad Wesley Smith's The Juggernaut Method 2.0, with the 10s and 8s waves inverted (many sets × few reps) as in his JTS article. "+" sets that stop short of failure, one test-day AMRAP per wave, double-progression accessories. Three quick steps to get you training.

Your 1-Rep Maxes

Enter the heaviest weight you can lift for exactly 1 rep with good form. If you don't know, use a recent heavy set and the calculator tool later. Be conservative — the whole program is built on this being honest.

Ready to Train

You're all set. Each training max (TM) starts at 90% of the 1RM you entered (book), and every prescribed weight comes from it. From now on the TM is the number that moves after each test; you can see and edit it in Settings.
